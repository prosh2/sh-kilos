# Product vision

## One line

Kilos makes it easy for endurance athletes to find people to train with.

## Who it's for

Runners, cyclists, swimmers, triathletes, duathletes, and other endurance athletes who want company for their training — from beginners looking for a no-drop group to experienced athletes looking for people who match their pace.

## The problem

Group training today happens in scattered chat groups and word of mouth. If you're new to a sport or an area, or your usual group doesn't match your pace or schedule, it's hard to find a session that fits.

## MVP — the Kilo loop

1. **Create** — an athlete posts a Kilo: sport, date/time, meeting point, distance, expected pace or intensity, and any notes.
2. **Browse** — others see upcoming Kilos and filter by sport, date, distance from them, and pace.
3. **Join** — an interested athlete joins the Kilo.
4. **Discuss** — participants talk in the Kilo's group to sort out the details.

The MVP succeeds if athletes who join a Kilo actually turn up and train together.

## Non-goals for MVP

Out of scope until after the MVP ships, so we don't get distracted by them:

- Recording activities in the app (GPS) or importing them (Garmin, Strava, HealthKit, FIT/GPX)
- Training logs, stats, plans, or analytics
- Segments, leaderboards, kudos on activities
- Clubs and organisations as entities (a club can still post Kilos as a normal user)
- Payments or paid events
- Web app

## Principles

- **Safety first.** People meet strangers in person. Default to protecting athletes' privacy and give them control over who sees what.
- **Fit matters more than volume.** A Kilo is only useful if the pace and distance match, so make those fields clear and easy to filter on.
- **Lightweight for hosts.** Posting a Kilo should take under a minute.

## Open questions

- Launch market (Singapore first?) — affects time zones, units, and moderation load
- Can anyone join a Kilo straight away, or must the host approve them? See the roadmap's open decisions
